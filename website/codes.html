DRYAD sheets and pigpen

Two old-school paper codes, built into Your Community Communications so a group can share keys, pick channels and pass short coded notes without any extra gear.

What DRYAD is

A DRYAD sheet is a table of scrambled alphabets, one per row, used to code numbers and pick shared secrets.

DRYAD comes from military radio procedure. Each sheet has rows labelled A to Z. Every row holds the alphabet in a random order, split into ten columns headed 0 to 9. Everyone in the group carries the same sheet, and each sheet is only valid for a set period, so it can be thrown away and replaced.

The classic paper sheet leaves out Z. Your Community Communications uses Modern DRYAD: every row holds all 26 letters plus two special symbols, 28 in all, shuffled together. The two specials stand for a space and a new line. They default to . (space) and @ (new line), and you can pick others, such as brackets, commas or semicolons, when you generate a batch. The columns hold 4, 3, 3, 3, 2, 3, 2, 3, 3 and 2 symbols.

Coding numbers

Agree a row. For each digit, send any one symbol from that digit's column. With row A above, 47 could go as YN, OG or YU. The receiver looks up which column each one sits in. Using a different symbol each time makes repeats harder to spot.

Shared secrets

A whole row is 28 random symbols that only your group holds. Your Community Communications can use a row as a channel name, so picking "row K" on today's sheet puts everyone on the same encrypted channel without saying the name out loud.

Keying pigpen

A row can also scramble the pigpen alphabet (below), so the same symbols mean different letters on every row.

What pigpen is

A centuries-old cipher where each letter becomes a shape cut from a grid or an X.

Draw two tic-tac-toe grids and two X shapes, and fill them with letters. The second grid and second X get a dot in each space. Each letter is then written as the outline of the space it sits in, plus the dot if it has one. Your Community Communications adds two more symbols in the centres of the X shapes: a plain X is a space, and an X with a dot in each corner is a new line. Classic pigpen fills the spaces with A to Z in order, then space and new line. In Your Community Communications you can instead fill them with a Modern DRYAD row, so the key changes with the row.

. in the key means a space and @ means a new line.

Pigpen is a simple letter swap. Keying it with a DRYAD row stops anyone who only knows classic pigpen, but a long message can still be broken by letter counting. Use it for short notes, and use the encrypted radio channels for anything that matters.

Using them in the app

Open the menu on the radio screen and choose DRYAD sheets or Pigpen.

1. Generate sheets

DRYAD sheets → Generate. Give the batch a friendly name, choose how many sheets, how long each one is valid and when the first starts, pick the two special symbols (or keep . and @), and leave "Secure random" selected. Each sheet gets a colour so they're easy to tell apart.

2. Share them

Share sends the batch as text through any app, shows one QR code per sheet for others to scan, or uses Send on air to send them over channel A or B to everyone on it. Receivers confirm the import, and sheets they already hold are skipped. Sheets can't be sent on Public.

3. Pick the sheet in use

The Sheet in use drop-down selects the currently valid sheet. Tap Mark used when you're done with one. Under Age rules you can delete sheets as soon as they're used, or a set number of hours after they expire.

4. Row to channel

On a row, tap Row → Ch A or Row → Ch B. That row's symbols become the channel name, so everyone who picks the same row on the same sheet lands on the same encrypted channel.

5. Make pigpen

Pigpen → choose the sheet and row (or Classic (A–Z)). Under Make pigpen, type the message and the symbols appear, with spaces and new lines as their own symbols, ready to copy by hand or screenshot.

6. Decode pigpen

Choose the same sheet and row, then use Decode (tap symbols). The pad is laid out like the figures (# # X X), so tap each symbol where you see it, including the X centres for space and new line. Delete and Clear fix mistakes, and the text appears as you go.

PIN lock. If a PIN is set (menu → PIN lock…), the DRYAD and pigpen screens ask for it first. They then stay unlocked for 5 minutes.
Emergency sheets. The generator can also rebuild sheets from a phrase, for when you can't share real ones. Anyone who guesses the phrase can rebuild every sheet, so the app marks them "(EMERGENCY)". Replace them with random sheets as soon as you can.
